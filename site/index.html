Moat Test: is NEAR Intents' revenue defensible?

Most NEAR theses ask how much Intents earns. An investor's next question is whether it can keep earning it. This page tests that three ways, each against a control built from competitors, so a market-wide move is never mistaken for a NEAR-specific one. The rules and thresholds were pre-registered (THESIS.md, results.json) and committed before any result was computed.

Verdict: Weak moat evidence (1 of 3 tests passed)

T1 Pricing power PASS

+16%

Volume vs control in the 8 weeks after the fee switch. NEAR's cut of fees went from 7.8% to 13.8%.

T2 Trust shock FAIL

-21%

Volume vs control in weeks 4–5 after the Oct 1 exploit (placebo p = 0.05). Not robust to the choice of controls.

T3 Price premium FAIL

14.0 vs 18.4 bps

Median fee rate, Intents vs rivals. Intents was more expensive in only 4% of 26 weeks: it wins on price, not premium.

What this means for an investor

  • The fee switch did not cost volume. NEAR raised its share of fees and volume kept pace with competitors. Taking a cut has not driven integrators away, so far.
  • Intents is the cheaper venue, not the premium one. Its users pay less than on native cross-chain rivals. That is a growth strategy, not evidence of a moat: a cheaper rival could compete the same way.
  • Trust is the open risk. Volume trailed its control by weeks 4–5 after the exploit. The signal passes the pre-registered test but is fragile under robustness checks; it is the number to watch.

Context

What it shows: Intents grew from almost nothing to a large venue in 2025; growth flattened in 2026. Both events tested here sit on the mature part of the curve.

Source: DefiLlama near-intents daily volume, summed to Monday–Sunday weeks; weeks with fewer than 4 reported days are left blank.

T1 Pricing power: the fee switch, Feb 23, 2026 PASS

On Feb 23, 2026 NEAR Intents switched on a protocol cut of the fees integrators charge. If integrators or users had cheaper alternatives, volume should have fallen relative to competitors.

What it shows: the switch was real. NEAR's share of fees rose from 7.8% in the 26 weeks before to 13.8% in the 8 weeks after, while the fee rate users paid stayed level (18.0 bps before, 17.6 bps after).

Source: DefiLlama near-intents daily revenue ÷ daily fees, weekly. Because the user price did not move, T1 measures whether integrators and their flow left, not users' price sensitivity.

What it shows: in the 8 weeks after the switch Intents ran +16% relative to its synthetic control. The pre-registered rule fails T1 only on a loss worse than −10% with p < 0.10, so T1 passes.

Synthetic control: weights on competitors' log weekly volume fitted on the 26 weeks before the event (Maya Protocol 63%, SOCKET 37%). Pre-period fit error (RMSPE) is 0.57 log points, which is loose: Intents' 2025 hypergrowth is hard for any mix of mature competitors to match.

Read this with care. 100% of the 29 placebo windows show a larger gain than the real one (median +430%), because they sit in 2025, when Intents was outgrowing everything. So the fee switch coincided with Intents' growth lead shrinking. The T2 placebo series below shows that lead had already fallen towards zero by January 2026, weeks before the switch, so the slowdown is not evidence that the switch caused it.

T2 Trust shock: the exploit, Oct 1, 2026 FAIL

On Oct 1, 2026 NEAR Intents was exploited for about $3.8M through a deposit-notification bug. The contract was paused within about 30 minutes, deposits resumed on Oct 2, and the funds were returned. A venue with loyal users should see volume come back within weeks.

What it shows: no immediate collapse (weeks 0–1: -1%), then Intents trailed its control by -21% in weeks 4–5. Week by week: wk 0: -25%, wk 1: +29%, wk 2: -14%, wk 3: -3%, wk 4: -24%, wk 5: -18%.

Synthetic control fitted on 26 pre-event weeks (Chainflip 1%, 0x Bridge Aggregator 18%, All DEX volume 81%); pre-period RMSPE 0.13, a close fit. Recovery statistic = mean gap in weeks 4–5.

Each dot re-runs the T2 estimator at a fake event date. In 2025 Intents outgrew every control, so those placebos sit far above zero; that is why the 2026-only view below matters.

What it shows: against all 63 placebo windows, p = 0.05. Using only the 34 placebo windows dated 2026, after the hypergrowth phase, p = 0.09. Both are below 0.10, so the pre-registered rule fails T2.

Why this is fragile. Dropping a single control moves the estimate between -63% and +12%. Adding the contaminated aggregators gives +27%. In the placebo-in-space test, Intents' post-event deviation ranks 5 of 5: less unusual than its own controls'. And the shape is not a typical trust shock: there was no drop in the first two weeks. Treat this as a warning signal to re-test, not a settled loss.

T3 Price premium, May 14, 2026 to Nov 11, 2026 FAIL

A business with a moat can charge more than rivals and keep its customers. This test compares what users pay per dollar swapped on Intents with what they pay on native cross-chain rivals.

What it shows: Intents' median fee rate was 14.0 bps against 18.4 bps for competitors; Intents was dearer in only 4% of 26 weeks.

Source: DefiLlama daily fees ÷ daily volume, weekly, for each venue. Competitor rate is volume-weighted across THORChain, Chainflip and Maya for the venues reporting that week (grey lines).

What it shows: Intents' share against Chainflip went from 74% to 83%; trend -0.08% a week (90% CI -0.45% to +0.34%), so share is holding. Against THORChain and Chainflip over the 20 weeks both report, the trend is -1.94% a week. That broader comparison points to share slipping against the larger rival, so “holding” applies to Chainflip only.

Share uses only competitors that report every week of the window, because a denominator that gains and loses venues would move on its own. T3 needs both a premium and steady share; the premium is missing, so T3 fails.

Limits

  • All volume and fee data comes from DefiLlama, an aggregator. Its history can be revised; the snapshot hashes below pin the exact version analysed.
  • The control pool is small (6 clean series), so placebo-in-space p-values cannot go below about 1/6.
  • Solver concentration is not measured: a 200-transaction sample of recent intents.near activity contained no token_diff swap events, so solver identity is not observable reliably from public data without the Explorer API.
  • Six weeks of post-exploit data is the most the snapshot allows. T2 should be re-run as data arrives; python3 -m moat refresh then build does exactly that.
