Is NEAR Intents' revenue defensible?

Three pre-registered causal tests of NEAR Intents' moat, each against a control built from competitors, checked against 3,346 NEAR blocks read directly from the chain.

Dydex · Data snapshot Nov 12, 2026 · Demo video · Code · Pre-registration · Thesis · All numbers (JSON)

Abstract

Most NEAR theses measure how much NEAR Intents earns. An investor's next question is whether it can keep earning it. This note tests that three ways, each against a synthetic control built from competing venues, so a market-wide move is never mistaken for a NEAR-specific one. The questions, data rules and pass/fail thresholds were committed to git before any result was computed.

Key findings for an investor

  1. The fee switch did not cost volume. NEAR raised its share of fees and volume kept pace with competitors. Taking a cut has not driven integrators away, so far.
  2. Intents is the cheaper venue, not the premium one. Its users pay less than on native cross-chain rivals. That is a growth strategy, not evidence of a moat: a cheaper rival could compete the same way.
  3. Trust is the open risk. Volume trailed its control by weeks 4–5 after the exploit. The signal passes the pre-registered test but is fragile under robustness checks; it is the number to watch.

1Question and design

Revenue is only worth a high multiple if it is defensible. NEAR Intents now earns real fees, so the question is whether it has a moat: can it raise its take, survive a loss of trust, and charge more than rivals, without losing volume?

Each test compares Intents with a synthetic control: a weighted mix of competitors (THORChain, Chainflip, Maya, SOCKET, 0x and the all-DEX market) fitted to Intents' log weekly volume over the 26 weeks before an event. The effect is the gap that opens after it. To judge whether a gap is unusual, the same estimator is re-run at every earlier fake event date (a placebo test). LI.FI, Rango and Bitget route part of their flow through Intents, so they are kept out of the control and used only as a sensitivity check. Full rules: METHODS.md.

2Pricing power: the fee switch ✓ PASS

Event Feb 23, 2026 · Estimate +16% volume vs control over 8 weeks · Rule fail only on a loss worse than −10% with p < 0.10

On Feb 23, 2026 NEAR Intents switched on a protocol cut of the fees integrators charge. If integrators or their users had cheaper alternatives, volume should have fallen relative to competitors.

Because the price users paid did not move, T1 measures whether integrators and their flow left, not users' price sensitivity.

Read this with care. 100% of the 29 placebo windows show a larger gain than the real one (median +430%), because they sit in 2025, when Intents was outgrowing everything. So the fee switch coincided with Intents' growth lead shrinking. The T2 placebo series (Section 3) shows that lead had already fallen towards zero by January 2026, weeks before the switch, so the slowdown is not evidence that the switch caused it.

3Trust shock: the exploit ✕ FAIL

Event Oct 1, 2026 · Estimate -21% volume vs control in weeks 4–5 · Placebo p 0.05 · Rule fail only on a loss worse than −10% with p < 0.10

On Oct 1, 2026 NEAR Intents was exploited for about $3.8M through a deposit-notification bug. The contract was paused within about 30 minutes, deposits resumed on Oct 2, and the funds were returned. A venue with loyal users should see volume come back within weeks.

Why this is fragile. Dropping a single control moves the estimate between -63% and +12%. Adding the contaminated aggregators gives +27%. In the placebo-in-space test, Intents' post-event deviation ranks 5 of 5: less unusual than its own controls'. And the shape is not a typical trust shock: there was no drop in the first two weeks. Treat this as a warning signal to re-test, not a settled loss.

4Price premium ✕ FAIL

Window May 14, 2026 to Nov 11, 2026 · Estimate 14.0 vs 18.4 bps · Rule dearer in ≥75% of weeks and share not falling

A business with a moat can charge more than rivals and keep its customers. This test compares what users pay per dollar swapped on Intents with what they pay on native cross-chain rivals.

T3 needs both a premium in at least 75% of weeks and a share that is not falling. Verdict: ✕ FAIL

5On-chain check: reading NEAR blocks directly

Sample 3,346 NEAR blocks from neardata.xyz, keyless, seeded and pinned · Status descriptive, not used for any verdict

Every test above uses DefiLlama, an aggregator. To check it against the chain itself, the build reads a deterministic sample of NEAR blocks and counts what intents.near executed in each one: signed intents (intents_executed events), swaps (token_diff events) and receipts. A weekly total is the mean per sampled block times the number of blocks in that week.

5.1 Does the aggregator match the chain?

5.2 The two events, on-chain

Fee switch. Executed intents in the 8 weeks after Feb 23 were -3% against the 8 weeks before; DefiLlama volume was -17%. In this window the week-to-week correlation of the two is 0.22.

Exploit. On-chain activity moved the same way, far more strongly: executed intents in weeks 4–5 after Oct 1 were +88% against the 8 weeks before, while DefiLlama volume was +7% over the same weeks.

These are before/after comparisons with no competitor control, because competitors' activity lives on other chains, so they are not comparable with T2's synthetic-control gap: both raw measures can rise while Intents still loses ground to rivals. Counts of signed intents are not dollars, and one strong week (Nov 5–11) drives most of the on-chain rise.

5.4 Who fills the swaps?

Each swap settles as a token_diff between a user and a solver. Users rarely appear in more than one sampled block; solvers do. Over the last 8 weeks, 13 accounts appeared in swaps in at least 3 different sampled blocks.

6The long-term case for NEAR

NEAR holders are diluted by new issuance; Intents revenue is what can offset it. Revenue is volume × fee rate × NEAR's cut, and the three tests say which of those levers NEAR can pull.

7Limits

  • All volume and fee data comes from DefiLlama, an aggregator. Its history can be revised; the snapshot hashes in Section 8 pin the exact version analysed.
  • The control pool is small (6 clean series), so placebo-in-space p-values cannot go below about 1/6.
  • The on-chain figures come from a sample of blocks, not a full census, so each weekly estimate carries sampling error (bootstrap intervals are in results.json). The sample is pinned in data/onchain/ and can be checked against the chain with python3 -m zeat verify-onchain.
  • Six weeks of post-exploit data is the most the snapshot allows. T2 should be re-run as data arrives; python3 -m zeat refresh then build does exactly that, and a daily job republishes this page on fresh data.

8Reproduce

Python 3.10+, standard library only, no API keys. The build is offline and byte-deterministic: it must reproduce this page, results.json and THESIS.md exactly.

git clone https://github.com/Dydex/zeat && cd zeat
python3 -m zeat verify     # data files match their sha256 hashes
python3 -m zeat build      # rebuilds every number, this page and THESIS.md (~40 s)
git status --short         # empty: the rebuild matches the committed files
python3 -m unittest        # known-answer and byte-exact rebuild tests
